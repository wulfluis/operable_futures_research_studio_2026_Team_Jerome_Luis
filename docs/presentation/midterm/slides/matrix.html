SECOND LOOK · FRAME 10 / 12

(45 s) This is the risk matrix behind the rules. Rows are the crack type: map cracking, bending, shear, cracks along a bar, column cracks, spalling and joints. Columns are the width: under one millimetre, one to three, over three. Each cell gives the starting category: one means recheck in six months, two recheck in one month, three means an engineer now. That is only the start. On top come the element, where the crack sits: a column, a beam, a slab or a wall. And the building survey: which storey, when it was last maintained, whether floors were added without a permit, and how old it is. [The animation: first the element answer raises some cells by one category, others stay; then the building survey does the same. The cells are illustrative.] Together they give the final category; how much each answer raises it, we define with a structural engineer. The thresholds are illustrative until a structural engineer calibrates them.
